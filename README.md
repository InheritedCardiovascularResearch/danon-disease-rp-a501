# Danon Disease and RP-A501

Research and clinical development resources on Danon disease, LAMP2 biology, RP-A501, and cardiac gene therapy.

## Overview

Danon disease is a rare X-linked inherited disorder caused by pathogenic variants in the **LAMP2** gene. The disease can affect multiple organs, with cardiac involvement representing one of its most serious clinical manifestations.

This repository organizes publicly available research and clinical development information around the relationship between:

**LAMP2 → Danon disease → cardiac disease → RP-A501 → clinical development**

The repository focuses on the biology of Danon disease, the role of LAMP2 and LAMP2B, the development of RP-A501, and the current clinical status of the program.

RP-A501 is an investigational gene therapy being developed by **Rocket Pharmaceuticals** for Danon disease.

This repository is intended as a research reference and does not provide medical advice or represent the position of Rocket Pharmaceuticals.

## Danon Disease

Danon disease is a rare X-linked genetic disorder associated with pathogenic variants in the **LAMP2** gene.

LAMP2 encodes lysosome-associated membrane protein 2, which has an important role in lysosomal function and autophagy.

Danon disease can involve the heart, skeletal muscle, and central nervous system. Cardiac manifestations can include hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, conduction abnormalities, arrhythmias, and progressive cardiac dysfunction.

The clinical course can be particularly severe in affected males.

Cardiac disease is therefore a major focus of current Danon disease research and therapeutic development.

---

## LAMP2 Biology

The **LAMP2** gene encodes lysosome-associated membrane protein 2.

LAMP2 is expressed in tissues including cardiac muscle, skeletal muscle, and brain and participates in cellular processes involving lysosomal function and autophagy.

Pathogenic LAMP2 variants can disrupt these processes and lead to abnormal accumulation of cellular material.

In cardiac muscle, disruption of LAMP2-related cellular function can contribute to the structural and functional abnormalities associated with Danon disease.

Understanding this relationship is important when evaluating therapies designed to restore LAMP2-related function.

---

## LAMP2B

LAMP2 produces different isoforms, including **LAMP2A, LAMP2B, and LAMP2C**.

LAMP2B is particularly relevant to cardiac gene therapy research.

RP-A501 consists of a recombinant **AAV9** capsid containing a functional version of the human **LAMP2B** transgene. The therapy is administered as a single intravenous infusion and is designed to deliver the functional LAMP2B gene to cardiac tissue.

Rocket Pharmaceuticals has reported that RP-A501 targets cardiac cells, including cardiomyocytes, and delivers the functional LAMP2B gene to heart tissue.

---

## RP-A501

**RP-A501** is an investigational gene therapy being developed by Rocket Pharmaceuticals for the treatment of Danon disease.

The program uses an AAV9 vector containing a functional LAMP2B transgene.

The proposed therapeutic sequence can be summarized as:

**LAMP2 mutation**

↓

**Functional LAMP2B gene delivery**

↓

**Myocardial LAMP2 protein expression**

↓

**Potential change in cardiac structure and function**

↓

**Potential clinical benefit**

This sequence represents the therapeutic rationale being investigated and should not be interpreted as an established clinical outcome.

The distinction between biological activity and clinical benefit remains important. Demonstrating expression of the therapeutic protein does not by itself establish that a treatment provides meaningful clinical benefit.

Clinical development therefore requires assessment of molecular, structural, functional, and safety outcomes.

---

## Clinical Development

RP-A501 has been evaluated in early clinical development and is currently being advanced through a pivotal Phase 2 program.

The clinical development program has evolved following safety findings and subsequent discussions with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration.

The current program uses a recalibrated dose, an optimized immunomodulatory regimen, enhanced eligibility criteria, additional safety monitoring, and risk-mitigation measures.

The recalibrated RP-A501 dose is **3.8 × 10¹³ genome copies per kilogram (GC/kg)** using commercial-grade product.

The modified immunomodulatory regimen includes rituximab, sirolimus, and corticosteroids.

The current approach was established following FDA review and alignment on continued enrollment and dosing under the modified protocol.

---

## Phase 1 Findings

Longer-term follow-up from the earlier Phase 1 study has provided clinical data supporting continued development of RP-A501.

At approximately 12 months, all six patients in the principal Phase 1 analysis demonstrated myocardial LAMP2 protein expression and at least a 10% reduction in left ventricular mass index.

At approximately three to seven years of follow-up, left ventricular mass index remained below baseline in five of six patients, with reductions of at least 10% in four patients.

Rocket Pharmaceuticals also reported sustained reductions in cardiac biomarkers and improvements in quality of life among the six patients.

These findings are from the earlier Phase 1 experience and should be distinguished from outcomes that will be generated in the current pivotal Phase 2 population.

---

## Current Pivotal Phase 2 Trial

The current pivotal Phase 2 study is registered with **ClinicalTrials.gov as NCT06092034**.

The study is a global, single-arm, multicenter Phase 2 pivotal trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of RP-A501 in male patients with Danon disease.

The current pivotal population includes **12 male patients**, with a pediatric safety run-in included in the study design.

A natural history comparator is also part of the current development framework.

The pivotal study is intended to evaluate whether RP-A501 can produce predefined biological and cardiac responses while continuing to assess safety and other clinical measures.

---

## FDA Alignment and Modified Protocol

In September 2026, Rocket Pharmaceuticals announced alignment with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration on continued enrollment and dosing and key elements of the pivotal Phase 2 trial.

The FDA confirmed:

- A 12-patient pivotal efficacy population
- Male patients receiving RP-A501 at the recalibrated dose
- Continued enrollment and dosing under the modified protocol
- Established 12-month co-primary endpoints
- A potential accelerated approval pathway based on the specified response criteria

The recalibrated dose of **3.8 × 10¹³ GC/kg** was selected following analytical characterization of the commercial-grade Phase 2 product and was supported by nonclinical bridging data.

In a Danon disease mouse model, Rocket reported that the recalibrated dose produced cardiac LAMP2B protein expression comparable to that observed at a higher dose, together with comparable vector biodistribution.

---

## October 2026 Clinical Status

On **October 7, 2026**, Rocket Pharmaceuticals provided an updated report following its October 6 Danon disease investor webinar.

The current pivotal efficacy population includes **12 male patients** receiving commercial-grade RP-A501 at the recalibrated dose.

The first three patients treated under the modified protocol **count toward the 12-patient pivotal population**.

This leaves **nine additional patients** to be enrolled and treated.

Rocket expects to complete pivotal trial dosing by **mid-2027**, with topline results anticipated in **mid-2028**.

The first three patients treated under the modified protocol completed the protocol-specified initial observation period without clinical or laboratory evidence of thrombotic microangiopathy or capillary leak syndrome.

Rocket has emphasized that these observations are preliminary and that follow-up remains ongoing.

---

## Why Was the Protocol Modified?

The current RP-A501 development program follows safety findings from the earlier Phase 2 experience.

Rocket reported that the original-dose study included serious safety events involving **capillary leak syndrome**, with thrombotic microangiopathy also reported. These findings informed the recalibrated dose, revised immunomodulation regimen, and enhanced safety monitoring now incorporated into the modified protocol.

Rocket's 2026 regulatory disclosures state that the earlier safety events led to a temporary clinical hold and subsequent modification of the treatment approach.

The FDA later authorized resumption of the study using the recalibrated dose and modified protocol.

The first three patients treated under the modified protocol subsequently completed the initial observation period without clinical or laboratory evidence of thrombotic microangiopathy or capillary leak syndrome.

These observations remain preliminary because follow-up is ongoing.

## Pivotal Trial Endpoints

The current pivotal Phase 2 study uses two co-primary response criteria assessed at 12 months.

### 1. Myocardial LAMP2 Protein Expression

The first criterion is myocardial LAMP2 protein expression of at least **Grade 1**, measured by immunohistochemistry.

This endpoint is intended to provide evidence of biological activity in cardiac tissue.

### 2. Left Ventricular Mass Index

The second criterion is a reduction of at least **10% from baseline in left ventricular mass index (LVMI)**.

LVMI provides a measure of cardiac structure and can be used to assess changes in abnormal left ventricular enlargement.

### Response Threshold

Rocket has stated that at least **seven of the 12 patients** must meet both response criteria at 12 months for the predefined success threshold to be achieved.

The co-primary endpoints are intended to support a potential accelerated approval pathway.

Additional secondary measures include changes in cardiac biomarkers and longer-term clinical and safety outcomes.

---

## Why LAMP2 Expression and LVMI Matter

The two primary measures address different parts of the treatment response.

**Myocardial LAMP2 expression** addresses whether the therapy is producing the intended molecular effect in cardiac tissue.

**LVMI** addresses whether there is a measurable change in cardiac structure.

Together, the measures create a development framework connecting the biological mechanism of the therapy with a structural cardiac outcome.

However, a molecular or structural response does not automatically establish overall clinical benefit.

Longer-term follow-up and additional clinical and safety measures remain important to understanding the ultimate effect of treatment.

## Natural History Study

Natural history data are important in rare diseases because patient populations can be small and disease progression can be difficult to characterize.

Rocket is sponsoring a global, multicenter natural history study for Danon disease alongside the pivotal RP-A501 program.

The study is designed to collect longitudinal prospective and retrospective clinical information and characterize the natural history of Danon disease.

The natural history program is intended to provide an external comparator for the pivotal Phase 2 study and help place treatment outcomes in the context of the underlying disease course.

Current trial and natural-history information should be verified against ClinicalTrials.gov and subsequent Rocket Pharmaceuticals disclosures as the programs develop.

## RP-A501 and Rocket Pharmaceuticals

RP-A501 is part of Rocket Pharmaceuticals' broader cardiovascular gene therapy strategy.

Rocket's cardiovascular portfolio includes three clinical-stage programs targeting different inherited cardiomyopathy subtypes:

| Program | Disease | Development stage |
|---|---|---|
| **RP-A501** | Danon disease | Pivotal Phase 2 |
| **RP-A601** | PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy | Phase 1 |
| **RP-A701** | BAG3-associated dilated cardiomyopathy | Phase 1 |

The programs address different genetic and biological causes of inherited cardiovascular disease.

RP-A501 addresses Danon disease associated with **LAMP2** deficiency.

RP-A601 addresses **PKP2-associated arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy**.

RP-A701 addresses **BAG3-associated dilated cardiomyopathy**.

Together, the programs form the core of Rocket Pharmaceuticals' cardiovascular gene therapy development strategy.
